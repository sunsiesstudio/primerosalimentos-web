Iron for babies: why it matters so much and which foods have it

By Martina Solés · 27 November 2026 · 4 min read

Around 6 months, the iron stores your baby was born with start to run out. That's why, from the very first day of weaning, iron is the priority.

Why it matters so much

Iron is needed to make red blood cells and for brain development. Your baby is born with a store that lasts until roughly 6 months. From then on, milk no longer covers everything they need and it also has to come from food.

That's why the AEP recommends offering foods rich in iron and zinc every day or almost every day from the moment they start eating.

Where to find the most iron

There are two types of iron in food:

  • Iron from animal sources (the most easily absorbed): beef, lamb, lean pork, chicken or turkey thigh, fish and egg yolk.
  • Iron from plant sources: lentils, chickpeas, beans, peas, tofu, oats and wholegrain cereals.

Ideally, every main meal should include one of these sources, well cooked and with the right texture for their age.

How to help them absorb it

  • Pair it with vitamin C. Tomato, red pepper, broccoli, kiwi, strawberries or orange help absorb plant iron. Lentils with tomato, or chickpeas with pepper followed by a few segments of mandarin for pudding, make a great combination.
  • No cow's milk as a drink before one. As well as being low in iron, it makes iron harder to absorb.
  • Keep meat juicy. In large strips to suck on, shredded or minced into soft meatballs. That makes it easier for them to eat some.

Easy ideas for every day

  • Red lentil and squash soup.
  • Baked beef meatballs.
  • Omelette strips.
  • Salt-free hummus on toast.
  • Shredded chicken thigh with vegetables.
  • Flaked hake or salmon, bones removed.

Do they need a supplement?

Most healthy babies born at term don't need a supplement if they eat iron-rich foods. In some cases (premature babies, low birth weight, certain diets) the paediatrician may recommend one. Don't give them supplements on your own: too much iron is harmful too.

FAQ

How much meat does a baby need to eat?

Not much at all. What matters is offering iron-rich foods almost every day; they decide how much to eat.

Do lentils have as much iron as meat?

They have quite a lot, but plant iron is harder to absorb. Serving them with foods rich in vitamin C, like tomato or pepper, helps a lot.

How do I know if they're low on iron?

Only a blood test can confirm it. If they seem very pale, tired or irritable, mention it to your paediatrician.
