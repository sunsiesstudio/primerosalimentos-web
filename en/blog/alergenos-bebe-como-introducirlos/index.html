Allergens for babies: when and how to introduce them

By Martina Solés · 9 November 2026 · 5 min read

For years the advice was to delay them. Today we know that doesn't help: introducing allergens early and in small amounts may help reduce the risk of allergy.

Early, in small amounts and without delay

Potentially allergenic foods can be offered from the very start of solids. Delaying them beyond 6 months doesn't prevent allergy, and offering them early may help reduce the risk.

The most common ones

  • Egg, always well cooked.
  • Fish, with no bones.
  • Peanut and tree nuts, always as a butter or finely ground, never whole.
  • Gluten: bread, pasta, oats (unless gluten-free).
  • Milk and dairy: plain yoghurt or fresh cheese. Cow's milk as a drink, after 12 months.
  • Others: sesame (tahini), soy, shellfish, some fruits.

How to introduce them

  1. One at a time. One new allergen at a time, so you know which one it was if there's a reaction.
  2. A few days in a row, in small amounts. For example, 3 days in a row.
  3. Ideally in the morning and on a weekday, so you can watch them during the day and easily reach your paediatrician.
  4. Once it's tolerated, keep it in their diet regularly.

How to offer some of them

  • Egg: hard-boiled in strips, or a well-set omelette.
  • Peanut: 100% peanut butter spread very thinly on toast, or stirred into yoghurt or mashed fruit.
  • Fish: hake or white fish, flaked and boneless.

If there's a reaction

Mild (hives, itching, redness, a single bout of vomiting): don't offer it again, write it down and check with your paediatrician.

Severe (swollen lips, tongue or face, difficulty breathing, repeated vomiting or very floppy): call 112 (the emergency number in Spain and the EU) and keep them lying down and watched until help arrives.

FAQ

Can I give egg at 6 months?

Yes, well cooked. There's no need to wait.

What if there are allergies in the family?

Check with your paediatrician before introducing that food, in case they'd rather do it differently.

How many days should I wait between one allergen and the next?

The usual approach is to offer each allergen for a few days in a row (for example, 3) before introducing the next one.
