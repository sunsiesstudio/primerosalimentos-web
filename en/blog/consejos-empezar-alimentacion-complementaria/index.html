10 tips for starting solid foods

By Martina Solés · 13 November 2026 · 5 min read

Learning to eat is a learning curve for your baby and for you. These ten tips make the first months easier, safer and a lot calmer.

1. Always sitting upright

Back straight and facing the table. Never reclined, not in a bouncer or a pushchair, and always with an adult next to them.

2. They decide how much they eat

You decide what to offer and when; they decide whether to eat and how much. This is called responsive feeding: respecting their hunger and fullness cues. No forcing, no rewards and no "just one more spoonful".

3. Milk is still the main thing

Until their first birthday, breast or bottle stays on demand. At first they'll eat very little, and that's completely normal.

4. Iron almost every day

From 6 months their iron stores drop. Offer meat, fish, eggs or legumes almost every day, well cooked.

5. No salt, no sugar, no honey

No added salt or sugar, and no honey before 12 months. For flavour, use herbs and mild spices.

6. Lumps early on

If you start with purées, make them thick and gradually lumpier. Don't keep going with smooth purées beyond 8 or 9 months.

7. Allergens early and one at a time

Egg, fish, nut butters or gluten can be introduced early and in small amounts, one at a time and for a few days in a row so you can watch for reactions.

8. Repeat, repeat, repeat

Refusing broccoli today doesn't mean they don't like it. Sometimes it takes many tries before they accept a new food. Keep offering it with no pressure.

9. Eat together, no screens

Babies learn by copying. Sitting them at the table with you, with no TV or phone, helps them eat mindfully and try what they see on your plate.

10. Accept that it'll get messy

Touching, squashing and throwing food is part of learning. A good long-sleeved bib and something under the high chair will save you a lot of grief.

FAQ

How many times a day should they eat at first?

At first, offering food once or twice a day is enough, with no pressure. Meals increase with age and appetite.

What do I do if they don't want to eat anything?

Nothing. Take the plate away calmly and offer again at the next meal. Milk still covers their needs.

Can I give them the same food we eat?

Yes, adapting the texture and with no added salt or sugar. Set their portion aside before salting yours.
