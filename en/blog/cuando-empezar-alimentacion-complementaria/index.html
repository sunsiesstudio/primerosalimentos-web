When to start solids

By Martina Solés · 6 November 2026 · 4 min read

The recommendation is to start at around 6 months, and never before 4. But rather than watching the calendar, watch your baby: there are clear signs they're ready.

At what age do you start?

The Spanish Association of Pediatrics (AEP) recommends starting solids at around 6 months. Starting before 4 months is never recommended, and it's also best not to delay much beyond 6, because from that age the baby's iron stores start to drop.

Every baby has their own pace, so the exact date matters less than these signs.

The 4 signs they're ready

  1. They can sit with little support and hold their head steady.
  2. Is interested in what you eat: they watch you eat, open their mouth, try to grab your food.
  3. They pick things up and bring them to their mouth.
  4. They no longer push food out with their tongue (the tongue-thrust reflex has gone).

If your baby shows these signs at around 6 months, it's a good time to start. If you're unsure, talk to your paediatrician.

Milk is still the main thing

It's called complementary feeding because it complements, it doesn't replace. Until age one, breast milk or formula is still their main food, on demand. At first they'll eat very little, and that's completely normal: they're learning flavours, textures and how to chew.

What about water?

From when they start solids you can offer water in a cup with meals, without pressure. They decide how much to drink.

Which food do I start with?

There's no set order. What does matter is that from the start there are foods rich in iron and zinc almost every day: meat, fish, egg or pulses, well cooked. Around them, vegetables, fruit and cereals, wholegrain where possible.

Allergens (egg, fish, nut butters, gluten, dairy) can be introduced early and in small amounts: delaying them beyond 6 months doesn't prevent allergy.

FAQ

Can I start at 4 months?

The Spanish Association of Pediatrics (AEP) recommends starting at around 6 months and never before 4. If you're thinking of starting before 6, check with your paediatrician.

Should they start with fruit or cereals?

There's no set order. What matters is including foods rich in iron and zinc from the start.

How much should they eat at first?

Very little. Milk is still their main food until their first birthday and solids are a complement. They decide how much to eat.
