Gagging or choking in babies

By Martina Solés · 6 November 2026 · 4 min read

It's one of the biggest fears when starting solids. The good news: gagging is normal and protects your baby. Knowing how to tell it apart from choking gives you real peace of mind.

Gagging is normal

When a baby starts eating, their gag reflex sits further forward in the mouth than an adult's. That's why they gag often: it's a protective mechanism that helps them push forward what they can't swallow yet.

How to recognise gagging:

  • They cough and make noise.
  • Their eyes water.
  • They push the food forward.

What to do: stay calm. Don't put your fingers in their mouth or pat them, as you could push the food further in. Let them cough.

Choking is something else

When a baby is choking, the airway is blocked. The signs are:

  • No coughing, no crying, no breathing.
  • They turn blue.
  • They make a high-pitched noise or are silent.

This is when you need to act now.

What to do if they choke (under 1)

  1. Call 112 or ask someone to call.
  2. 5 back blows: face down along your forearm, with their head lower than their body.
  3. 5 chest thrusts: face up, with two fingers in the centre of the chest.
  4. Repeat until the object comes out. If they lose consciousness, start CPR.

Taking a paediatric first aid course is the best way to be prepared, whatever method you use.

How to prevent it

  • Always sitting upright, never reclined, not in a bouncer or a pushchair.
  • Always with an adult next to them and no screens.
  • Grapes, cherry tomatoes and blueberries: quartered lengthways, never whole.
  • Nuts: never whole before age 5, only as a butter or finely ground.
  • Apple and carrot: cooked until soft, or finely grated.

FAQ

Is it normal for my baby to gag a lot?

Yes. At first the gag reflex sits further forward and protects them. It usually lessens as they learn to eat.

Does BLW increase the risk of choking?

When it's done safely (soft foods, the right shape, and the baby sitting up and supervised), studies haven't found more choking than with purées.

Should I put my finger in their mouth if they gag?

No. You could push the food further in. Stay calm and let them cough.
