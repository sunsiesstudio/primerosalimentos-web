What a baby can't eat before one (and until when)

By Martina Solés · 18 November 2026 · 3 min read

Almost everything can be offered from 6 months, but there are a few foods it's best to avoid. Here's the list, the reasons why and until when.

The list

  • Salt. Never add it. Their kidneys can't process it well yet and it gets them used to salty flavours.
  • Sugar and juices. They encourage tooth decay and get them used to sweet flavours. Whole fruit is better.
  • Honey. Never before 12 months: it can contain botulism spores, a serious form of food poisoning in babies.
  • Cow's milk as a drink. Not before 12 months: it has little iron and too much protein and salts for their kidneys.
  • Whole nuts. Because of the choking risk. Until age 5, only as nut butter or very finely ground.
  • Whole grapes, cherry tomatoes and blueberries. Always quartered lengthways.
  • Large fish. They contain a lot of mercury: swordfish, shark (tope, blue shark), bluefin tuna and pike.
  • Spinach, chard, beetroot, rocket and celery. They're high in nitrates. None before 12 months; from 1 to 3 years, one portion a day at most.
  • Borage. It's very high in nitrates. None before age 3.
  • Rice drink. It builds up inorganic arsenic: best avoided. Rice and rice cakes only now and then.

All of this until your paediatrician tells you otherwise.

What about allergens?

Allergens aren't on this list: egg, fish, peanut or gluten can be introduced early and in small amounts. Delaying them beyond 6 months doesn't prevent allergy. The usual approach is to offer them one at a time, for a few days in a row, so you can see how they go down.

FAQ

Can they have yoghurt or cheese before one?

Cow's milk as a drink is avoided before 12 months. Plain yoghurt and fresh cheese are usually introduced earlier; check the timing with your paediatrician.

Can I add a pinch of salt?

No. No salt is added to their food before one. For flavour you can use mild spices and herbs.

When can they have honey?

From 12 months.
